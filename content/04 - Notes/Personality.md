## What is Personality?

**Personality** refers to the long-standing traits and patterns that propel individuals to consistently think, feel, and behave in specific ways

**Phrenology** was an old method to determine personality by looking at bumps on the skull, has no evidence

Kant and Wundt produced the 2 axis emotion and temperament chart for personality
### Various Theories
#### Psychodynamic Perspective
- Sigmund Freud
- Unconscious motivation (iceberg theory)
- Divisions of the mind
	- Conscious
	- Preconscious
	- Unconscious
 - Personality
	 - Id (Impulses) <- devil on your shoulder
	 - Superego (Morals) <- angel on your shoulder
	 - Ego (Rational) <- compromises, if it can't then it pushes the issue into the subconscious
  - Defense Mechanisms
	  - Denial: refusal to accept real events because they are unpleasant
	  - Displacement: transferring inappropriate urges or behaviors onto a less threatening target
	  - Projection: attributing unacceptable desires to others (cheats because he probably is)
	  - Rationalization: justifying a behavior by substituting an acceptable reason for less-acceptable reasons
	  - Reaction Formation: reducing anxiety by adopting beliefs contrary to your beliefs
	  - Regression: returning to less mature coping strategies
	  - Repression: suppressing painful memories and thoughts
	  - Sublimation: redirecting unacceptable desires through socially acceptable channels

#### Humanistic Perspective
- Emphasis on human potential
	- free will
	- self-awareness
	- psychological growth
- Maslow's hierarchy of needs
	- In order to be the highest (self-actualization), you need to take care of the essential things first
- Carl Rogers
	- actualizing tendency
	- self-concept (congruence vs incongruence)
		- does what you do match what you say
	- the best way to help someone realize who they should be is to offer them **unconditional positive regard**

##### Criticism
- Difficult to test scientifically
- perhaps to rosy of a picture
#### Social Cognitive Perspective
- we have conscious thought processes
- capable of self-regulated behavior
- consequences and learning (good->more likely to do it again)
- Reciprocal determinism
	- Cognitive, behavioral, and environmental factors all influence each other
- Designed to be testable
#### Trait Perspective
- focused on describing and measuring personality
	- does not deal with causes or factors
- 3 Criteria
	- It should be consistent across different environments
	- It should be stable over time
	- It should be something that people can differ on
- Surface traits (something that we see a lot)
- Source traits (the foundation for someone's personality)
- Myers-Briggs Type Indicator
	- Most common personality test used by fortune 500 and government agencies
	- Very questionable about accuracy
	- Reminiscent of learning types
- 5 Factor Model (OCEAN)
	- Openness (trying new things)
	- Conscientiousness (perseverance, orderly, hard working)
	- Extraversion (energetic and social, how you recharge - different than social anxiety)
	- Agreeableness (go with the flow)
	- Neuroticism (more likely to experience ups and downs especially negative emotions)

## Personality Tests

### Self Report Tests
- Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI) (answer a bunch of questions about yourself)
	- MMPI-2-RF
### Projective Tests
- Rorschach Inkblot Test (what do these blobs mean?)
- Thematic Apperception Test (tell a story about these pictures)
- Rotter Incomplete Sentence Blank (finish the sentence with what you are thinking about)
