## 1-16: Holiness & Sacrifice
**Ritual Sacrifices**
- Burnt, grain, fellowship, purification, and restitution offerings (Lev 1–7).
- Sacrifices atone for sin and restore communion with God (CCC 2100).

**Priesthood**
- Aaron and his sons ordained as priests (Lev 8–9).
- Priests act as mediators between God and Israel.
- Strict holiness required in approaching God.

**Purity Laws**
- Clean vs unclean (Lev 11–15).
- Ritual impurity restricts access to worship.
- Emphasizes God’s holiness and human separation due to sin.

**Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur)**
- Annual atonement for all Israel (Lev 16).
- High priest enters the Holy of Holies.
- Scapegoat carries away sins.
- Prefigures Christ’s once-for-all sacrifice (Heb 9; CCC 433).

## 17-27: Holiness in Life
**Holiness Code**
- “Be holy, for I am holy” (Lev 19:2).
- Moral, social, and religious laws govern daily life.

**Love of Neighbor**
- “Love your neighbor as yourself” (Lev 19:18).
- Foundation of moral law, fulfilled in Christ (CCC 1822).

**Faithfulness to the Covenant**
- Blessings for obedience, consequences for disobedience (Lev 26).
- Covenant relationship requires fidelity.

**Worship & Sacred Order**
- Sacred times, vows, and offerings ordered to God (Lev 23–27).

## Core Theological Takeaways
- God is **perfectly holy** (CCC 208).
- Sin separates man from God → requires **atonement**.
- Sacrifice restores communion but is **fulfilled in Christ**.
- The priesthood mediates between God and man → fulfilled in Christ.
- Holiness includes both **worship and moral living**.
- Leviticus prepares for **the perfect sacrifice of Jesus**.
