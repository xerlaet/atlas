# State and Local Government Relations
---

Since the U.S. Constitution doesn't mention local governments, it is up to the states to decide and various methods are currently implemented

States have **unitary government** where the central government has the power for revoke or override actions taken by local governments. Local governments are commonly organized into counties and municipalities

Local governments can either be under **general law** where they have to follow the state law, or **home rule** where they can create a charter and expand on laws and regulations following the overall state laws

In addition there are **special-purpose governments** that can span across multiple counties, such as school districts. In 2014, there were fourteen special-purpose governments devoted to mosquito control. Special-purpose governments are a way of cementing power for special interest groups

The **environmental justice movement** argues that the environmental costs of an industrialized society should be spread more equally among those who benefit from it

**Consolidation** is the process of merging overlapping units of local governments to reduce redundancy and increase efficiency. Since it often involves reducing officials, current incumbents are often opposed to it. A more common process is **annexation** where two units of government at the same level are merged, such as with unincorporated land and an existing municipality

The **regional planning council** is in charge of discussing issues with metropolitan areas
