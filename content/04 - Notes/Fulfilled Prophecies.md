# Fulfilled Prophecies
---

Psalm 22 and Zechariah 12:10 describe the Messiah's hands and feet being pierced, bones pulled out of joint, and executioners casting lots for his clothing.

Isaiah 13:19-20 and Jeremiah 51 declared that Babylon (at the time in it's height of power) would be conquered and eventually completely uninhabited. To this day it is still a ruin (although there was a failed attempt in the 1980s to rebuild Nebuchadnezzar's palace)

Isaiah 34, Ezekiel 35, and Obadiah mention that Edom would become a perpetual wasteland. They were driven out and their land remains a barren desert today.

Ezekiel 26 mentions that Tyre (a wealthy Phoenician coastal fortress) would have multiple nations coming against it like ocean waves. Specifically, Nebuchadnezzar would break the walls, and the city would be thrown into the middle of the sea, scraping the rock bare. Nebuchadnezzar did indeed besiege Tyre for 13 years, and later Alexander the Great arrived and took the ruins of the ancient mainland city and threw them into the sea to build a half-mile bridge to the island to walk across and destroy it.

## Analysis
Many of the prophecies could have been taken out of their original context because they happen to fit (e.g. Isaiah 7:14 the virgin birth, Isaiah 53 the suffering servant)

Jesus should have known these prophecies, perhaps he convinced Himself that He was the Messiah, and could simply fulfill a lot of them Himself (being crucified, riding in on a donkey)

The prophecies mentioned can't be explained by those counters! Although the part about eventually an empire falling is just basic geopolitical theory. I believe Isaiah 13 prophesied that Babylon would be overthrown as a sudden, fiery cataclysm however it fell peacefully. Ezekiel 26:14 says that Tyre will never be rebuilt, however Tyre (Sur) is currently the fourth largest city in the country.

So maybe some of these prophecies failed, maybe some succeeded. Perhaps they were written after the events themselves? Probably unlikely but possible, we don't have as great of a record of the OT as we do the NT.

Are there any failed prophecies in the Bible? I think of the claim that I believe Paul says where he claims there will be people among those living now who will not die before experiencing the new Heaven, which depending on your interpretation did or didn't happen.

## Stance
I need to read the Bible a lot more, but I'm skeptical that a lot of these prophecies were either taken out of context or fulfilled by Jesus intentionally believing He was the Messiah. And for the ones unrelated to Jesus, they were either educated guesses, were predicted after the date, got lucky, and people in the future like Alexander decided to retrofit for fun. At least I can see some combination of those as being plausible, but I know there are a lot so I will have to look into this more.
