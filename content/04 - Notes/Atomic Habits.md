# Atomic Habits
---

You don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems. The most effective way to change your habits is to focus on who you wish to become, as your identity emerges from your habits.

The core idea is that small, consistent changes compound over time to produce remarkable results.

## The Habit Loop
All habits follow a four-step neurological loop:
1. **Cue**: the trigger that initiates behavior
2. **Craving**: the motivational force or desire
3. **Response**: the actual habit or action
4. **Reward**: the satisfying outcome that teaches the brain to repeat the loop

## The Four Labs of Behavior Change
To build a good habit:
1. [[Make it Obvious]] (cue)
2. [[Make it Attractive]] (craving)
3. [[Make it Easy]] (response)
4. [[Make it Satisfying]] (reward)

To break a bad habit:
1. Make it **Invisible**
2. Make it **Unattractive**
3. Make it **Difficult**
4. Make it **Unsatisfying**

## Advanced Tactics
### Specializing
Everyone is born with different abilities based on our genes. The trick is to pick the right field of competition where you have an advantage. Similarly, choose the habit that best suits you

One of the best breakdowns of personality traits is the OCEAN (openness, conscientiousness, extroversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism).

In the beginning of a new activity, there should be exploration to try out many possibilities. Over time, shift focus to the best solution but keep experimenting occasionally

What is fun to you that seems like work to others? What lets you get into [[Flow]]?

When you can't win by being better, win by being different. Also the more specialized you become the harder it is for others to compete

### Progressive Overload
We experience peak motivation when working on tasks right at the edge of our current abilities -Goldilocks rule or [[Progressive Overload]]. So start with an easy behavior, and over time keep advancing it in small ways

Increasing difficulty like this can also keep things interesting. One of the greatest threats to success is not failure, but boredom. Increasing difficulty just enough so you have to keep working can keep things engaging

### True Mastery
The upside of habits is that we do things without thinking. The downside is that we stop paying attention to little errors. Habits + Deliberate Practice = Mastery

Reflection and review is a process to remain conscious of performance over time

Keep your identity small to be able to adapt easily (e.g. what happens once FTC is over?)
