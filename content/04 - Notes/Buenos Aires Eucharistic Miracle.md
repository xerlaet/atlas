# Buenos Aires Eucharistic Miracle
---
![Pasted image 20260927192723.png](/attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260927192723.png)
On August 18th, 1996 a parishioner noticed a consecrated host discarded and left on a candle holder at the back of the church. The priest placed it into a receptacle of water inside the tabernacle to dissolve naturally. Eight days later, he opened the tabernacle and found that it had transformed into a bloody, fleshy substance.

The priest informed the later to be Pope Francis who ordered several photographs taken. The host remained in distilled water, where it did not decay for several years.

In 1999, Dr. Ricardo Castañón Gómez was brought in. He took a fragment of the bloody mass and sent it to various laboratories in New York. The scientists, most notably Dr. Frederick Zugibe, were not told anything about the sample.

Dr. Zugibe's analysis yielded the following:
1. The substance was human myocardium from the left ventricle
2. The tissue showed signs of severe inflammation, consistent with a heart that has suffered extreme stress
3. Many intact white blood cells were found, indicating it was alive
4. The tissue showed no signs of decay

Additional analysis by genetic laboratories revealed:
5. Human DNA was present, however a full genetic sequence was unable to be captured (Does this indicate a miraculous origin or standard DNA degradation?)

Finally, to this day the host has not dissolved. The results of the investigation were never submitted to a peer-reviewed scientific journal, but primarily published in Dr. Castañón's books and Catholic media.

## Analysis
**Time Gap**: From 1996 to 1999, the sample was kept in a tabernacle only overseen by parish priests.

**Chain of Custody**: It is possible someone swapped the dissolving bread with a piece of actual cardiac tissue? Something to note is that white blood cells die incredibly fast (often within 15 to 30 minutes). This means the forger would have had to harvest the heart from a patient who just died and very quickly place it into a chemical fixative to keep the white blood cells.

**Investigator Bias**: Dr. Ricardo Castañón Gómez had been researching and validating Catholic mystical phenomena for years prior to Buenos Aires. Dr. Frederick Zugibe was a devout Catholic and one of the world's leading religious forensic experts. Both of these people would be sympathetic to a miracle claim. However, it should be noted that Zugibe testified he didn't know it was supposed to be a miracle when he made his diagnosis. On the other hand, Dr. Castañón has published at least 16 books on investigations into miracles and is a full time speaker now, having delivered over 1,000 conferences across five continents.

**Lanciano Connection**: This sample matches with Lanciano, being both from the left ventricle and sharing the AB blood type. Whoever did this would likely have to have known about the details of the Lanciano miracle.

## Skeptical Theory
In 1996 the host dissolved in water over three years, with bacterial contamination making the appearance of a bloody substance. In 1999, someone swapped the actual material for a small, freshly acquired piece of cardiac tissue from a recently deceased hospital patient. The sample was then immediately placed into a chemical fixative (like formalin which is common to preserve biological samples). Dr. Castañón then handed this over to Dr. Zugibe, who correctly identified it as a traumatized human heart.

Because of the chemical fixative, the human DNA was fragmented and would yield the unreadable genetic results reported. Once the scientific investigation was over, the human heart tissue was dried out and placed into a reliquary, and as such was able to be preserved to this day.
