# Cosmological Argument
---

1. Whatever begins to exist has a cause ([Principle of Sufficient Reason](/notes/principle-of-sufficient-reason))
2. The universe began to exist (big bang)
3. Therefore the universe had a cause (space-less, time-less, immaterial)

## Analysis
For premise 1, does the principle of sufficient reason hold (that everything which exists needs a cause or reason to exist)? Not sure. Although science does seem to rely on it.

For premise 3, the cause doesn't have to be God. Who knows if there is another answer for what started everything. Before we had quantum physics or general relativity, no one would have thought that was the case. Maybe it could be something similar for any non material things (Wolfram's idea, some better formulation of the multiverse, etc)

Does everything require something to continuously sustain their existence?

How do abstract entities like "2+2=4" fit into the picture? Are they independent from some necessary first cause?
- perhaps it is a structural relation of the first cause's intellect
- perhaps abstract objects don't really "exist" and are shortcuts/conventions
- perhaps God created logic itself and could have made "2+2=5"
- perhaps there are multiple necessary entities
	- although if A and B are necessary and distinct in some way, that means they have limits of some sort and can't fully explain themselves. By [PSR](/notes/principle-of-sufficient-reason), this calls for an explanation

## Stance
The argument for a singular necessary entity without limits is compelling, and I think PSR is true or at least we should accept it, although I do think we have some severe epistemological limits here.

Ultimately, I think it works however I'm skeptical that the Christian God is the answer. I don't see a good reason from this alone to think that the cause is personal or omnibenevolent or has agency. I'll leave that to the other arguments
