## 1-11: Solomon's Reign
**Solomon Becomes King**  
- David dies; Solomon anointed king (1 Kgs 1–2).  
- Begins reign with wisdom and devotion to God.  

**Wisdom and Wealth**  
- Solomon asks for wisdom; God grants both wisdom and wealth (1 Kgs 3).  
- Famous for fair judgments (e.g., the two mothers and the baby).  

**Temple Built**  
- Solomon builds the temple in Jerusalem (1 Kgs 6–8).  
- God’s presence dwells among Israel; central place for worship.  

**Solomon’s Downfall**  
- Marries foreign wives → idolatry introduced (1 Kgs 11).  
- God promises kingdom division as a consequence of disobedience.  

## 12-16: Israel Splits in Two
**Division of the Kingdom**  
- Rehoboam’s harsh policies provoke northern tribes → Israel splits (1 Kgs 12).  
- North: Israel; South: Judah.  

**Northern Kings**  
- Jeroboam sets up golden calves → idolatry entrenched.  
- Cycle of sin: kings do evil, prophets warn, judgment delayed.  

**Southern Kings**  
- Judah’s kings sometimes faithful (e.g., Asa) but often fail.  
- God’s covenant with David persists in Judah despite failures.  

## 17-18: Israel's Kings vs. The Prophets
**Prophetic Confrontation**  
- Elijah rises to challenge Baal worship (1 Kgs 17–18).  
- Demonstrates God’s power through miracles and Mount Carmel showdown.  

**Cycle of Warning**  
- Prophets confront kings: Ahab and Jezebel.  
- God calls Israel back from idolatry repeatedly.  

## 19-17: The Road to Exile
**Continued Rebellion**  
- Israel persists in idolatry → God’s patience tested.  
- Prophets like Elisha continue warnings and miracles (2 Kgs 2–13).  

**Assyrian Threat**  
- Northern kingdom increasingly vulnerable to foreign powers.  
- Prophets warn of consequences: exile and judgment inevitable.  

## 18-25: Jerusalem's Demise and Babylonian Exile
**Judah’s Decline**  
- Kings of Judah alternate between faithfulness and idolatry.  
- Reforms (e.g., Hezekiah, Josiah) temporarily restore worship.  

**Babylonian Conquest**  
- Judah falls to Babylon (2 Kgs 24–25).  
- Jerusalem destroyed, temple burned, people exiled.  

## Core Theological Takeaways
- **Obedience to God is central** for national and personal flourishing.  
- Leadership matters: wicked kings lead to societal collapse.  
- God is patient but **justice is inevitable**.  
- Prophets reveal God’s will and call for repentance.  
- Promises to David’s line persist → points forward to Christ.
