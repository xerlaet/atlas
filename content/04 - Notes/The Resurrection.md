# The Resurrection
---

Here is a list of minimal facts that skeptics and believers alike agree on:
- Jesus died by crucifixion
- His tomb was found empty (75% of scholars agree)
- Shortly after his death, several of Jesus's followers experienced appearances of Jesus after his death
- His earliest followers came to believe that He rose from the dead (including skeptics like Paul and James)
- Several of his followers were [martyred](/notes/martyrdom-of-the-apostles) for their beliefs

The best explanation for the data is that Jesus was raised from the dead and appeared physically to the apostles.

## Analysis
Secular historians like Thallus and Phlegon of Tralles wrote about an inexplicable darkness and massive earthquake occurring in the 30s AD. Although apparently this is coming from later Christian apologists? Is it possible this was a literary trope with how cosmic events would be paired with the deaths of great figures?

Paulogia's minimal witness hypothesis: [Link](https://www.bartehrman.com/minimal-witnesses-hypothesis/)
1. Jesus was an apocalyptic preacher in Judea
2. Jesus was crucified
3. The resting place of Jesus was unknown to his followers
4. Most of the followers disappeared from reliable history
5. Peter experienced a post-bereavement hallucinatory like experience
6. James, the brother of Jesus, joined the movement
7. Stories about Jesus spread through person-to-person evangelism
8. Paul experienced a vision of the allegedly-resurrected Jesus
9. Paul met Peter in Jerusalem and didn't see eye-to-eye
10. Several decades later, Greek-speaking individuals documented the stories about Jesus
11. Peter and Paul were killed by Nero as scapegoats for the fire in Rome, and James was killed in a power grab by a new high priest
12. Christianity became Rome's official religion, marking its transformation into what we know of it today

James Fodor's [RHBS Model](/notes/rhbs-model)

Possibly the earliest source we have for Christian beliefs is the creed that Paul gives in Corinthians (which may have been 3-5 years after the crucifixion):

> For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures, and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers at one time, most of whom are still alive, though some have fallen asleep. Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles. Last of all, as to one untimely born, he appeared also to me
> - 1st Corinthians 15:3-8

Something to note here is that while it may be implied, it doesn't explicitly mention the empty tomb. Is it possible they thought Jesus was raised only spiritually and the appearances mentioned here are all spiritual and not physical?

## Stance
I think there are possible naturalistic explanations for the resurrection, likely some combination of the minimal witnesses hypothesis combined with the RHBS model, and a little bit of fanciful storytelling. However I still grant that these alternate explanations can seem like a stretch and rely at times on ad-hoc assumptions of what would have needed to happen.
