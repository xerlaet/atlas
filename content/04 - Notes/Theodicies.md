# Theodicies
---

**Theodicy**: An attempt to explain why God allows evil and suffering

## Agency & Moral Value
- **Free Will Defense**: The genuine capacity to freely choose between good and evil is of supreme importance. Hence moral evil and suffering from it must be allowed.
- **Soul Making**: Humans were created to grow into divine likeness. The world is filled with obstacles, risks, and hardships that enable humans to develop deep virtues.

The free will defense works well for moral evils. The soul making theodicy seems problematic, because if a child dies from a disease, it doesn't help that child at all. If the child's suffering is only meant to teach others, then it seems God was using them as a tool.

## Structural & Physical Necessity
- **Natural Law**: We need a universe that is stable and has predictable natural laws to make choices and act meaningfully. However, fixed physical rules means that sometimes they will result in harm without constant, chaotic divine intervention.
- **Evolutionary Necessity**: Perhaps God wanted to create a universe capable of "making itself" through evolutionary processes and emergent complexity. Because biological evolution relies on mutation, natural selection, competition, and physical limitations, suffering and decay are unavoidable.
- **Higher Order Goods**: In order for higher-order goods (e.g. bravery) to exist, the world must contain the structural possibility and actualization of lower-order failures and pains.

These arguments seem to be unimaginative. Surely God could find a way to allow for predictable laws or a way for the universe to "make itself" without all of the suffering we experience? The higher order goods argument seems compelling, however a world with 10% of the current rate of suffering would likely be enough for humans to learn higher-order goods. Also though, in Heaven there is no suffering, yet are people still brave there?

## Cosmic & Spiritual Order
- **Fallen World**: Natural evils are a result of spiritual rebellion. Either occurring from the original human Fall or demons.
- **Illusion Theodicy**: Evil is a deprivation of goodness. As we draw nearer to God, the perceived substance of evil and suffering dissolves.

The illusion idea does work I think, however it relies on an understanding of reality that is non intuitive. Attributing natural disasters and animal suffering to demons is an ad hoc assertion, but otherwise also works.

## Value Overpowering & Re-balancing
- **Aesthetic Theodicy**: The universe is not a utility-maximizing hedonistic machine, but rather a grand artistic masterpiece. Instead of maximizing the comfort of individual creatures, what matters is the holistic aesthetic value.
- **Infinitude of the Incarnation**: The incarnation and following death and resurrection is a good of infinite depth. Because it has infinite depth, it is worth any finite amount of creaturely suffering.
- **Afterlife Compensation**: For animals (and perhaps humans?) that suffer in this life, God compensates them in the next life balancing the cosmic ledger of justice.

The Aesthetic Theodicy seems to imply that God doesn't truly love individual creatures. The Incarnation being an infinite good seems to allow for any amount of finite suffering, which seems problematic. And compensation after the fact also seems problematic, because again it could allow for any amount of suffering.
