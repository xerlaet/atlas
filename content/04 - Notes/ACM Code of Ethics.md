# ACM Code of Ethics
---

Computing professionals (anyone who uses computing technology in an impactful way) impact the world, and hence should reflect on their actions to ensure their impact is positive.

Axiom: The public good is always the primary consideration

The code is not an algorithm to solve ethical dilemmas, it is a basis for ethical decision making

## 1. General Ethical Principles

### 1.1 Contribute to society and to human well-being, acknowledging that all people are stakeholders in computing
Promote fundamental human rights and autonomy, with a goal of minimizing negative consequences areas such as health, safety, personal security, and privacy. Give priority to the needs of the less advantaged.

Pro bono or volunteer work benefiting the public good is encouraged.

Supporting environmental sustainability both locally and globally is important to ensure a safe natural environment for the public good.

### 1.2 Avoid harm
Avoid harm (any unjust negative consequence). Consideration of long term outcomes should be taken into account.

If necessary to mitigate such risks, becoming a whistle blower is a responsible action to take

### 1.3 Be honest and trustworthy
Be transparent and provide full disclosure, and don't present false or misleading claims or accept bribes. Be honest about their qualifications, and honor commitments.

### 1.4 Be fair and take action not to discriminate
Foster fair participation of all people regardless of any defining factor. Design for inclusiveness and accessibility.

### 1.5 Respect the work required to produce new ideas, inventions, creative works, and computing artifacts
Developing new ideas, inventions, and works create value for society, and therefore the inventors of such should be credited and trade secrets and copyrights should be respected.

### 1.6 Respect privacy
Technology enables mass collection of private data. Policies for collecting data should be transparent and informed consent is necessary along with the ability for users to review, edit, and delete their personal data.

Only the minimal amount of personal information necessary should be collected

### 1.7 Honor confidentiality
Protect confidentiality except in cases of violation of law or other regulations.

## 2. Professional Responsibilities

### 2.1 Strive to achieve high quality in both the processes and products of professional work
The dignity of everyone should be respected through every process. Professionals should be cognizant of any serious negative consequences affecting stakeholders that may result from poor quality work and should always prioritize quality work.

### 2.2 Maintain high standards of professional competence, conduct, and ethical practice
Retain technical knowledge and always keep learning, independent study is a good idea

### 2.3 Know and respect existing rules pertaining to professional work
Abide by all local, regional, national, and international laws and procedures unless there is a compelling ethical justification to do so otherwise

### 2.4 Accept and provide appropriate professional review
Seek out and utilize peer and stakeholder review, and provide constructive reviews of other's work

### 2.5 Give comprehensive and thorough evaluations of computer systems and their impacts, including analysis of possible risks
Computing professionals are trusted and have a responsibility to provide objective and credible evaluations and testimony.

A system for which future risks cannot be reliably predicted requires frequent reassessment of risk as the system evolves, or it should not be deployed (important for AI)

### 2.6 Perform work only in areas of competence
Evaluate potential work assignments, and disclose any lack of expertise

### 2.7 Foster public awareness and understanding of computing, related technologies, and their consequences
Share technical knowledge with the public and foster awareness and understanding of it

### 2.8 Access computing and communication resources only when authorized or when compelled by the public good
Only in rare scenarios should a computing professional use unauthorized access to disrupt or inhibit the functioning of malicious systems

### 2.9 Design and implement systems that are robustly and usably secure
Security is important, so monitoring, patching, and vulnerability reporting are essential.

## 3. Professional Leadership Principles

### 3.1 Ensure that the public good is the central concern during all professional computing work
People should always be the central concern in computing.

### 3.2 Articulate, encourage acceptance of, and evaluate fulfillment of social responsibilities by members of the organization or group
Leaders should encourage participation in meeting social responsibilities

### 3.3 Manage personnel and resources to enhance the quality of working life
Enhance, not degrade, the quality of working life.

### 3.4 Articulate, apply, and support policies and processes that reflect the principles of the Code
Be consistent with this code

### 3.5 Create opportunities for members of the organization or group to grow as professionals
Make opportunities for improvement available to your team. Computing professionals should be fully aware of the dangers of oversimplified approaches, the improbability of anticipating every possible operating condition, and inevitability of software errors, interactions of systems, and other related issues.

### 3.6 Use care when modifying or retiring systems
Changing interfaces impact users, so investigate viable alternatives to removing support for a legacy system

### 3.7 Recognize and take special care of systems that become integrated into the infrastructure of society
Continuously monitor how society is using a system

## 4. Compliance with the Code

### 4.1 Uphold, promote, and respect the principles of the Code
The future of computing depends on technical and ethical excellence. Recognize breaches of the Code and take actions to resolve issues

### 4.2 Treat violations of the Code as inconsistent with membership in the ACM
Being in the ACM implies following the Code
