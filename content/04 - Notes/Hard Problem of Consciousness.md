# Hard Problem of Consciousness
---

How does the mind arise from a physical brain?

## Analysis
The problem is aptly named. Something to note though is that even with substance dualism, consciousness still seems really hard to understand and grasp.

At the very least we know that consciousness can be altered based on the alteration of our physical brain - it could be argued that with immaterial minds when your brain is half blown apart your mind shouldn't change, you should just suddenly feel like your body is failing you. But who knows.

Are AI models conscious? I don't think so, but does that mean that my intuition is telling me deterministic fully material entities will never be conscious, and that therefore we have to be non material?

Counter point, someone who is born blind apparently has know idea what color looks like, and people born deaf don't know what sound sounds like. So it seems that sensory qualia are completely dependent on physical experience. This connects to the [Mary's Room](/notes/marys-room) thought experiment.

Similarly, modern research shows that newly sighted individuals struggle to match objects known by touch to objects seen by sight, performing only slightly better than chance. Over time, however, their brains learned to connect the two senses.

[Split Brain Experiments](/notes/split-brain-experiments) are very strange. Just like the person who fabricated why they started walking, is our free will simply an illusion? An immaterial soul doesn't have parts so it couldn't be cut in half, yet the two brain halves appear to have different conscious desires in the case of the patient trying to grab two different shirts.

The dualist response is to say that the left hemisphere retains the single conscious soul (with the language center and interpreter), while the right hemisphere turns into a philosophical zombie. Your conscious soul is wrestling with a malfunctioning biological machine.

## Stance
It is a hard problem for naturalism. However, things like split brain experiments and how qualia is so dependent on physical experience make me think there must be a way consciousness can arise naturalistically. Is this strong enough evidence though to discredit the feeling of consciousness by the [Principle of Credulity](/notes/principle-of-credulity)? I'm not entirely sure.
