> [!important]
The slowest link will determine how fast packets are actually sent

**Circuit switching** gives dedicated frequencies or times for each user, meaning often lots of bandwidth is not used. Hence **packet switching** allows the network to be shared between everyone, but packets need to be selected in a fair way

Forwarding just sends a packet forward, routing sends a packet to a specific destination

**Internet service providers** (ISP) are the main backbone of the internet and connect access nets to each other. IXPs and regional ISPs are further connections

**Content provider networks** exist to serve things like Netflix at massive scales

**Throughput** is the rate at which bits are being sent from sender to receiver

**Round-Trip Time** (RTT) is the time it takes a packet to travel to a destination and back again

forwarding (switches) is implemented in hardware, routing (routers) use algorithms and tables in software

**Packet Switching** breaks messages into packages and sends them on demand, vs **Circuit Switching** where it guarantees performance but can support very few with wasted bandwidth
