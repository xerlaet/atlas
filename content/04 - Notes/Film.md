# Film
---

Originally films were black and white without sound, and gradually they evolved to include sound and color and are now commonly shot digitally

Mutual Film Corp. vs. Industrial Comm'n of Ohio (1915) ruled that films are not protected by the 1st amendment so they can be censored. This led to many different regulation boards, and Hollywood made their own (MPPDA)

Catholic League of Decency came about which had a lot of power, leading to the Hay's Code (1930)

Joseph Burstyn Inc. vs. Wilson (1952) rules that films actually are protected by the 1st amendment and cannot be censored. Due to this the production code was ignored, and was transformed into the MPAA with the modern rating system. It is not required for a film to be rated, however most theater chains won't show an unrated or NC-17 film

In 1947 the **House Committee on Un-American Activities** (HUAC) was established and investigated any communist activities throughout the US. This led to the **Hollywood 10** which were a group of filmmakers that were charged with contempt of Congress, as well as the **Hollywood Blacklist**

## What is a Film

Film Styles:
- **Classical** are films made under Hollywood, where the story is important
- **Realist** are where films don't necessarily focus on the story but instead on average, everyday characters
- **Formalist** disrupt cinematics, relying on unusual visual techniques
- **Avant-garde films** try to upset the standard formulas and try new things

Meaning of a film:
- descriptive (neutral like a summary),
- interpretive (makes an argument about what the film does)
- evaluative claims (belief on whether a film is good or bad)

An **ideology** is a set of values that is not necessarily shared by all people in a culture, but still shape how individuals think around the world

## Narrative Form
The Narrative Form is the organizing framework on how the film is told  

**Diegesis** is the implied world of the story, and everything that exists outside of that are **extradiegetic** (ex. opening crawl in Star Wars or narrations)

The term for a complete chronological story is a **fabula** and the abbreviated and often reorganized version that plays onscreen is known as the **syuzhet**. Flashbacks and flashforwards cut backward and forward in time. 

The **exposition** serves to introduce important information about the characters including justifying their idiosyncrasies

**Motifs** are repeated details to emphasize a point
**Parallels** are when two different elements parallel each other

Films commonly use a **three-act structure** in which act one introduces characters and goals ending with a turning point, in act two the protagonist faces obstacles culminating the the climax, and in act three conflicts are resolved. Sometimes a **four-part structure** is used where the film is split into four equal length segments, with a critical turning point in the dead center of the film

**Frame narration** is when a character narrates an embedded talk to implied listeners. Types of narration include first person, third person, omniscient, and restricted narration where one or more characters speak somewhat but aren't an explicit narrator

An **episodic narrative** is where events are not tightly connected in a cause-and-effect sequence. A film is **open-ended** when all conflicts are resolved

A commercial formula often used by Hollywood for movies strives for clarity for the viewers, unity between cause and effect, goal-oriented characters, closure in the final act, and unobtrusive craftsmanship so viewers don't remember they are watching a movie

A **point-of-view shot** is when the audience shares the visual perspective of a character, and **direct address** is when a character speaks directly to the camera

## Mise en Scene
Setting, human figure, lighting, and composition

Setting is either through constructed sets, or on location. Forced perspective is used to manipulate the viewer's eye, also CGI

The human figure is often similar to type casting when an actor always have certain roles. **Impersonation** is when an actor "becomes" their character, personification, method acting, character actors are supporting characters, and extras don't have any lines, cameos

A prop is anything an actor touches or interacts with, otherwise it is set dressing

Lighting has quality (hard or soft), placement, and contrast (high or low)
Hard is close to the subject, emphasizes imperfections, whereas soft light diffuses over a larger area. It can also just be natural light

The standard is **three point lighting**, the **key light** illuminating the primary source, the **fill light** to eliminate shadows from the key light, and the **back light** which separates the subject from the background

Composition is the visual arrangement of elements in the frame. This could be loose framing with lots of space or tight framing which is constricted

**Chiaroscuro** is contrast of extreme light and dark in the same shot, some colors can be more or less saturated

## Cinematography
Frame, shot (set of frames)  take is a version of a shot, storyboard (set of shots)

Types of shots: long takes, slow/fast motion, stop motion

Camera Space: offscreen, eye level, high angle, low angle, overhead, dutch/canted
Camera Distance: extreme long shot, long shot, medium long (knees), medium (waist), close up, extreme close up

Camera movements reveal info, establish perspective, convey a sense of space, suggest a mood, emphasize continuity of time and space

35mm, 70mm imax

special effects in camera, visual in post

## Editing
Editing is the process of combining shots together to derive meaning. The **Kuleshov Effect** states that shots derive meaning not just from within itself but from adjacent shots

A **collage** is a sequence of shots together which can show change. **Tempo** is achieved from the length of shots and type of transitions between shots to slow down or speed up the pace. **Timing** is placing transitions to coincide with other visual or sound elements

Cuts can jump into the future or suggest parallels between similar events

A **shot/reverse shot** cuts back and forth between two opposing angles usually when two people are talking
