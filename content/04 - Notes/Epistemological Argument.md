# Epistemological Argument
---

1. Rationality requires universal, immaterial, unchanging laws of logic. Under naturalism, abstract laws can't emerge from blind matter
2. Even if abstract logic exists, a purely physical brain cannot interact with it. Under naturalism, brain states are caused by matter not by rational decisions
3. Natural selection selects for survival behaviors, not truth-oriented behaviors. Therefore you cannot fully trust your mind

Since naturalism fails to account for the existence of logic, the mechanics of reasoning, and the evolutionary reliability of the mind, the worldview is epistemologically self-defeating. The skeptic has to use rational tools that their own worldview undermines.

## Analysis
We all assume logic and reasoning at the start in order to get anywhere

It would seem that a good deal of truth can be obtained from observation of the environment through our senses, and in some cases we really shouldn't trust ourselves (e.g. being drunk, tired, or emotionally biased)

Doesn't evolution select for truth-tracking faculties? "Oh there's a cliff. If I jump off I will hit the ground at a high speed and based on experience likely die. Hmm I don't want to die. Therefore I won't jump off the cliff"

Computers are deterministic, and can be programmed to deal with logic correctly. Why couldn't our brains do something similar?

## Stance
1. I think that logic can describe how reality naturally operates, they don't necessarily have to be immaterial objects floating around.
2. The brain can be wired to execute logic just like a computer. I agree that under naturalism we don't have a designer to guarantee it, but it seems plausible based on experience
3. Having systematic false beliefs seems like it will correlate with failure (death)
