# Therapy and Treatment
---
## Types of Treatment
### Psychoanalysis
In **free association** the patient relaxes and says whatever comes to mind

Freud believed that the ego would try to block certain urges, so a therapist would have to interpret the underlying meaning of the dream through **dream analysis**

**transference** is taking certain feelings and transferring them to someone else

Psychoanalysis is not very research supported
### Humanistic Therapy
The treatment is **client-centered**

focused on the client's self-concept, and be genuine, unconditional positive regard
### Play Therapy
It is often used with children since they are not likely to engage in a traditional talk therapy in a productive manner

The idea is that children play out their hopes, fantasies, and traumas while using toys

It can be both **directive** where the therapist interacts with the play session, and **nondirective** where the therapist just observes
### Cognitive-behavioral Therapy
Focuses more on present issues than on childhood or the past

Combines both behavioral and cognitive therapies together

**Rational emotive therapy (RET)** was one of the first forms of cognitive-behavioral therapy.

**Action Belief Consequence (ABC)** method
#### Cognitive Therapy
Focuses on how a person's thoughts leads to feelings of distress

The therapist will challenge irrational beliefs focusing on what makes them illogical

Ex. instead of saying I'm worthless and stupid because I failed a test, say I'm smart but I didn't study for the test, however I can do better in the future
#### Behavior Therapy
Rather than digging deeply into one's unconscious like psychoanalysis, behavior therapy focuses on helping the client to change undesirable behaviors by employing classical and operant conditioning

**Counterconditioning** is used as a classical conditioning technique
- **aversive conditioning** pairs and unpleasant behavior such as a mild electric shock when the client engages in the undesirable behavior such as smoking or drinking
- **ABA?**
- **exposure therapy** is where clients are directly presented with the object or situation they fear such as a rabbit until the client overcomes their fear
	- **systematic desensitization** is a method of exposure therapy where a person encounters a series of graduated exposures

## Treatment Modalities
An **intake** is the therapist's first meeting with the client, and is done to gather information and inform the client on what to expect and discuss treatment goals
### Individual Therapy
The client and clinician meet one-on-one usually weekly or bi-weekly
### Group Therapy
A clinician meets with several clients with similar problems

It can help decrease a client's shame and isolation about a problem, although at the same time members of the group may be afraid to share secrets with complete strangers and confidentiality could be a concern
#### Family Therapy
Family therapy is a special type of group therapy consisting of one or more families

The therapist often helps to resolve issues and boundaries within the families and overall promote clearer communication
#### Couples Therapy
A couple will have therapy together traditionally using cognitive-behavioral therapy

Often the goal is to work out a couples problems or determine whether they should stay together
## Evaluating Treatments
There have been studies that show CBT was equally effective or more effective than other therapies in treating PTSD, generalized anxiety disorder, depression, and social phobia, another study found that psychodynamic therapy was also as effective

No studies have shown that one approach is better than another or a relationship between the effectiveness and the level of the clinician's training or experience

One critical factor however is the relationship between the client and therapist

Also sometimes a person will have **comorbid disorders** meaning that the individual has two or more diagnoses

The health professional also needs to develop **cultural competence** where they understand and address issues of race, culture, and ethnicity

Barriers to treatment is also a concern as perceptions and attitudes and shape who seeks treatment
