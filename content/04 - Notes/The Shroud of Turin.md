# The Shroud of Turin
---

The **Shroud of Turin** is arguably the most studied artifact in human history. It is a 14-foot linen cloth bearing the faint, yellowish image of a naked, crucified man. For centuries it was venerated as the burial cloth of Jesus, but exploded into the scientific consciousness in 1898 when photographer Secondo Pia took the first picture of it.

Here are some of the miraculous properties:
- The image on the cloth is a photographic negative. A 14th century medieval artist would not have conceived of, let alone painted, a perfect photographic negative 500 years before photography was invented
- The shroud encodes 3D spatial depth through the light and dark values
- The image is not made of paint, dye, or pigment. The discoloration is caused presumably by rapid, premature aging of the cellulose only .2 micrometers deep (top 2-4 fibers)

## Skeptical Response
A carbon-dating measurement by three labs was done in 1988 dating the shroud between 1260 and 1390, which matches the first time it appears in the historical record (1354 in Lirey, France). Believers have said that the samples could have been medieval patchwork, or heavily contaminated later on

Skeptics argue that the anatomical proportions prove it is a piece of art, being roughly eight heads tall with elongated forearms and fingers which matches Gothic art conventions of 14th century Europe. Although after crucifixion, the muscles might be locked resulting in the tenting affect allowing the hands to rest lower. The elongated fingers could be an illusion from the bones showing through the skin under intense radiation.

There was an X-ray analysis done dating it to the 1st century. Although this is a novel method performed by a pro-Shroud advocate, and assumptions about temperature and humidity were made which might not be accurate.

Luigi Garlaschelli tried to reproduce the shroud by placing a linen cloth over a volunteer, rubbing it with acidic pigment, aftificially aging it in an oven, and washing the pigment off. This resulting in the same discoloration of the fibers with a 3D negative image. Theists will point out though that the image was 40 fibers deep, not 4. Also, there was still wraparound distortion of the face. So to this day, no one has made a convincing copycat.

There might be a link between the shroud and the Mandylion of Edessa - however skeptics point to the legendary development of that cloth
