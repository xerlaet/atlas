# Newton's Laws of Motion
---

**Newton's Laws of Motion** are the three foundational axioms of [Classical Mechanics](/notes/classical-mechanics) that describe the relationship between a physical body and the forces acting upon it. They form the basis for all [Dynamics](/notes/dynamics) calculations.

## The Three Laws
- **First Law (Inertia):** A body remains at rest or in a state of uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by a net external force. 
- **Second Law (Force):** The rate of change of momentum of a body over time is directly proportional to the net force applied. For systems with constant mass, this means force equals mass times acceleration.
- **Third Law (Action-Reaction):** When two bodies interact, they apply forces to one another that are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

## Governing Equations
The mathematical definitions of the second and third laws are expressed as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma\mathbf{F} &= \frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt} \\
\Sigma\mathbf{F} &= m\mathbf{a} \\
\mathbf{F}_{AB} &= -\mathbf{F}_{BA}
\end{aligned}
$$
