# Composite Historical Critical Model
---

Jesus grew up in a culture expecting a Messiah to crush the Roman rule. He saw that he had a Davidic lineage (relatively common in Judea) and was from the Galilee region.

As he studies the texts he sees them reflecting his own life, his baptism by John triggers a conviction that he is God's chosen eschatological agent.

In the ancient world with no modern medicine, severe stress, trauma, and poverty could manifest as mind-body ailments (often diagnosed as demon possession). A charismatic figure like Jesus could trigger massive placebo-effect healings. These healings could have been exaggerated before they were written down.

He then intentionally fulfills "controllable" prophecies, organizing the donkey ride, breaking the bread like Melchizedek, staying silent before Pilate, and being crucified.

Several prophecies just fall into place, Roman soldiers weren't going to split an expensive garment, and with Jesus dying sooner on the cross from being heavily scourged, they didn't need to break his legs (or Jesus could have let himself die sooner)

Jesus died on Friday before the Sabbath, and due to the law Jesus was placed in a nearby, convenient tomb temporarily. Once the Sabbath ended Saturday night, the burial detail returned and buried Jesus in a standard, unmarked criminal graveyard. This leaves Mary Magdalene and the women who went to the temporary tomb on Sunday morning confused. By the time Peter proclaimed the empty tomb and that Jesus was alive, perhaps Joseph of Arimathea was dead, moved elsewhere, lost political power, who knows

With the tomb being empty, Peter who is devastated experiences a severe bereavement hallucination (well-documented occurrence), interpreting this as a bodily resurrection. A social contagion is sparked, and the grieving disciples began experiencing similar visions. Paul  and James experienced visions from guilt.

James was stoned in 62 AD, Peter was crucified in Rome 64-67 AD, Paul was beheaded in Rome 64-67 AD
The story spreads, Christianity becomes the official religion of the Roman Empire, and it spreads around the globe.

## Other Notes
There were other Messianic movements in the 1st century (like Simon bar Kokhba, who actually kicked the Romans out of Jerusalem for three years, before the movement was crushed. However they didn't have the empty tomb or the same level of biblical fulfillment)

Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder (FND) is a real thing that people have been cured from suddenly. Many soldiers who suffered from PTSD following WWI were cured by aggressive suggestion, hypnosis, or even sudden electric shocks. Over 150 Cambodian refugee women who survived the Khmer Rouge were blind, yet their eyes were entirely healthy. A sudden intervention might be able to cure it. Up to 20% of people diagnosed with epilepsy have PNES - intense seizures triggered by underlying psychological distress, not brain misfirings, that in the ancient would would be diagnosed as demonic posession. Again, a sudden commanding authority could snap a person out of that state.

Perhaps legendary development could have extended the actual cures into cures for leprosy, walking on water, healings from a distance?

Within a few years of Alexander the Great's death (and while he lived), myths circulated that he was born of a virgin and was the son of Zeus
