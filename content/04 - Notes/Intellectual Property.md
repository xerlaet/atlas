# Intellectual Property
---

## Key Terms
- **Copyright:** A legal concept that defines rights to certain kinds of intellectual property, protecting creative works such as books, articles, songs, movies, and software.
- **Patent:** A legal concept that defines rights to intellectual property, protecting inventions, including some software-based inventions, by giving the inventor a monopoly for a specified period.
- **Intellectual Property:** Intangible creative work that is protected by copyright, patent, trademark, or trade secret law.
- **Fair Use:** A legal doctrine that permits limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research.
- **Derivative Work:** A new work based on a pre-existing copyrighted work, such as a translation, adaptation, or movie based on a book.
- **Public Domain:** The status of a work whose copyright has expired, allowing anyone to freely copy and use it.
- **Digital Rights Management (DRM):** Technological measures used by copyright holders to control access to and use of digital content.
- **Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA):** A U.S. law that, among other things, includes "anticircumvention" provisions prohibiting the use or distribution of tools designed to bypass technological measures protecting copyrighted works.
- **Reverse Engineering:** The process of analyzing a product to understand its design and function, often by deconstructing it; in software, it involves translating machine code into a human-readable form.
- **Warez:** A term for unauthorized copies of software.
- **Software Piracy:** High-volume, unauthorized copying of software. The term's scope has expanded to include unauthorized copying of other forms of intellectual property.
- **File-Sharing:** The practice of distributing or providing access to digital media, often through peer-to-peer networks.
- **Contributory Copyright Infringement:** Liability incurred by a party who knowingly encourages or assists in the infringement of copyrights by others.
- **First Sale Doctrine:** A legal principle stating that the copyright owner's right to control the distribution of a particular copy of their work is exhausted after the first sale of that copy.
- **Jailbreaking/Unlocking/Rooting:** Circumventing mechanisms in devices like smartphones or game machines that prevent the installation of unauthorized software or services.
- **Cyberlocker:** A service that provides storage of large files on the Web, often used for sharing files, which can be a platform for both authorized and unauthorized content distribution.
- **Free Software:** Software that provides users with the freedom to run, copy, distribute, study, change, and improve the software.
- **Copyleft:** An agreement used with free software that allows people to use, modify, and distribute the software (or any program developed from it) only if they apply the same agreement, ensuring derivative works remain free.
- **GNU General Public License (GPL):** A widely used copyleft license for free software.
- **Patent Troll:** A pejorative term for a company that accumulates patents, often without making any products, and primarily earns income by suing other companies for patent infringement.
- **Business Method Patent:** A patent that protects methods or processes used in business, including many innovations in e-commerce and Web technologies.
