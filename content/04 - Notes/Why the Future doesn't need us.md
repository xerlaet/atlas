# Why the Future doesn't need us
---

> Our most powerful 21st-century technologies - robotics, genetic engineering, and nanotech - are threatening to make humans an endangered species

Postulating that computer scientists succeed in developing intelligent machines that can do all things better than humans, either machines will make all their own decisions or human control will be retained.

In scenario one, eventually humans will likely become depending on the machines by so much that turning them off would amount to suicide. In scenario two it is up to the elite to determine what will happen since the rest of humanity will be superfluous. These ideas come from Theodore Kaczynski.

Hans Moravec writes about a free marketplace and how biological species almost never survive encounters with superior competitors.

We have yet to come to terms with the fact that 21st-century technologies - robotics, genetic engineering, and nanotechnology - they can self replicate in the physical world, and the ability to create them only requires some basic easy to access knowledge - knowledge enabled mass destruction (KMD)

With Moore's Law in full effect, computing power has reached/is reaching a point where the dreams of Kurzweil and Moravec can come true

Making assemblers comes with a large risk - the gray goo problem - if such an assembler even if it is just like grass or a bacteria outcompetes everything else it could make the earth uninhabitable

Atomic weapons are deadly

GNR - genetics, nanotechnology, robotics
