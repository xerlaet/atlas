# Sokolka Eucharistic Miracle
---
![Pasted image 20260927211002.png](/attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260927211002.png)
On October 12, 2008, at St. Anthony of Padua in Sokolka, Poland, a priest accidentally dropped a consecrated host during communion. Following protocol, he placed it in a small container of water inside the tabernacle to dissolve.

One week later, the container was opened and it was discovered that most of the host had dissolved, but floating or attached to it was a vivid, bright red, coagulated blood-like clot that appeared physically bound to the remaining fragment of bread.

In 2009, fragments of the material were submitted to independent medical experts at the Medical University of Białystok. Both professors assigned to the case (Dr. Maria Elżbieta Sobaniec-Łotowska and Dr. Stanisław Sułkowski) were given the samples completely blind. Their official findings concluded:
1. The structure was myocardium from the left ventricle
2. The myocardial fibers and remaining fibers of the wafer were deeply intertwined
3. Despite sitting submerged in water for over a week and drying out on a linen cloth, the tissue did not undergo normal cellular breakdown

## Analysis
**The Missing Blood Checklist**: The report focused on transmission electron microscopy of muscle tissue, rather than serological blood typing. Hence, it was not known if there were red or white blood cells present, or AB antigens.

## Skeptical Theory
The red clot was caused by mold or bacteria (such as *Monillia* or *Rhodotorula*). This also caused the appearance of intertwined fibers, mimicking striated tissue.

The lack of putrefaction is explained by the rapid drying out once placed on the linen corporal, which halted the bacterial activity.
