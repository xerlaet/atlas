P52 (containing John) and was found in the sands of Egypt hundreds of miles from Ephesus and was dated to 125 and 150 AD. This means John was probably written in 90s AD.

The earliest creed is dated to 1-5 years after the crucifixion summarizing the resurrection appearances. Paul's letters are dated roughly 20-30 years after the events. The Gospels are somewhat later (maybe idk lol). Living witnesses should be present to correct any errors or mistakes, and route memorization was prized

The Romans destroyed the Second Temple in Jerusalin in 70 AD. Matthew, Mark, and Luke have Jesus predicting that the Jerusalem Temple will be completely destroyed, with "not one stone left on another" This is either meaning those Gospels were written after 70 AD, or fortold it. It could be just predicting that the Temple will fall in the future, knowing the Romans. Still, if so that would put the gospels into the 50s and 60s AD.

Textual Criticism shows (by comparing the 5800+ ancient Greek manuscripts) that once the Gospels were written down, they were copied with astonishing, meticulous accuracy

## Response
Perhaps between 30 AD and 70 AD the stories of Jesus were passed around and minor details grew fuzzy, were forgotten, or were slightly exaggerated. The authors then took oral traditions and edited them to preach to their specific audiences - this can be seen with Mark being written first, and Matthew and Luke taking from it. For example, Matthew could smooth out mistakes "Jesus did not do many miracles there" instead of "Jesus could not do any miracles there in Nazareth because the people lacked faith", or adding apocalyptic, legendary embellishments to highlight the theological importance of the crucifixion
