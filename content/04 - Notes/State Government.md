# Socioeconomic and Political Context
## Three Levels of Government
The US has a **federal** structure (national government) with 50 state governments. Having both national and state governments is a **dual sovereignty** system. Each state also has local governments which will provide most public services.

![[Pasted image 20240911145217.png]]

Federal Government Responsibilities
- national defense
- foreign relations
- domestic regulations
- power to tax and spend
- regulates interstate commerce

All states have the basic tripartite structure of government however they vary in implementation

The overall idea of states is **laboratories of democracy** where states will try things out and ideally the best ideas are implemented by other states. Ex. California curtailed smoking

**Dillon's Rule** is the idea that local governments derive authority from and are subject to the state government

## Texas Constitution
There have been 5 different constitutions over the history of Texas

Approved in 1876 with 17 main articles
Amended 507 times

All Constitutions Include a protection of basic rights, division of powers, a governor, usually bicameral legislatures
- The average constitution in 42,000 words whereas the US Constitution in 7,591
- Overall the states have adopted 144 constitutions with more than 7,580 amendments
- equal rights for women, victims act, free school through 12th grade
- State constitutions can include more rights than the federal law

Federal Law supersedes state law, ex. same sex marriage 

The largest source of income is from the federal government, and sales tax

There is no income tax (reaffirmed in 2019) and no state-wide property tax

## Population Dynamics
The US has a population of 335 million people. California has approximately 39 million and Texas has an estimated 30.5 million. Comparatively, Wyoming has under 600,000 people.

**Urbanization** is the process of the population moving into cities
Pros
- more land available for agricultural and recreational uses
- higher entrepreneurial activity
- higher economic opportunity
- easier to create infrastructure
Cons
- heat islands
- higher housing costs
- traffic and congestion

Increased urbanization means an increased focus on services instead of good production

The **poverty line** is a fixed income amount that varies on the size of a family denoting whether someone is considered to be in poverty or not. It does not adjust to the cost of living and doesn't take into account preestablished wealth

**Political ideology** is the set of beliefs and opinions that someone has

Generally, conservatives will favor strict gun-control and abortion laws, oppose same-sex marriage, and support free-market policies while liberals will generally be opposite

A political party will have state control when it holds both legislative chambers and the governorship

**Police powers** are the powers that governments have to regulate behavior and practices.

The **Grange Movement** was a movement of farmers in the late 1800s which supported limited government and corporation (railroad) power

## Federalism
The relationships between states are referred to as **horizontal federalism**, whereas **vertical federalism** is the relationship between different levels of government

Federalism is similar to a **confederation** which is a way of organizing a government between equal sovereign units (Articles of Confederation where the government had to request taxes from the states). A **unitary** government is when the national government holds all the authority

The United States constitution consists of **enumerated powers** giving specific power to the federal government and leaving the rest to the states, commonly called **police powers** referring to health, safety, and welfare of citizens

| Federal Powers               | Shared Powers        | State Powers                 |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Coin money                   | Raise taxes          | Regulate education           |
| Raise an army                | Spend money          | Regulate health              |
| Declare war                  | Borrow money         | Regulate intrastate commerce |
| Create a postal service      | Take property        | Criminal justice             |
| Regulate immigration         | Charter corporations | Administer elections         |
| Make laws necessary & proper |                      |                              |

### Constitutional Provisions
The **Supremacy Clause** in article VI of the constitution holds that federal laws supersede state laws. Even the choice not to regulate something can be considered a federal decision

The **Necessary and Proper Clause** in article I allows for implied power for Congress

The **Commerce Clause** in article I gives Congress the power to regulate commerce between states and foreign nations and has been ruled to affect behavior that is not directly interstate commerce

The **Tenth Amendment** of the Bill of Rights reserves powers to the states that are not explicitly given to the federal government

### History
After the Great Depression where unemployment peaked at 25%, FDR became president and was able to use laws like the commerce clause to make sweeping policy decisions which the Supreme Court eventually upheld. LBJ continued this trend implementing Medicare and Medicaid

The government utilized different types of grants to help accomplish some of these goals. **Block Grants** are a form of grant-in-aid which gives the receiving entity full control of how to spend the money. A **matching grant** is when the government matches the money that the states spend at a fixed ratio.

## Local Governments
**Geographic Jurisdiction** is power granted over certain land, whereas **Subject Matter Jurisdiction** is a large set of things which a government has power over

The Constitution only lists federal and state governments, so local governments only exist because the states allow them to

The local government consists of counties and municipalities. Cities are said to have **home rule** when they can pass their own laws so long as they don't conflict with state laws. Otherwise they must follow the **general law** of the state, and can only pass pre-authorized laws

Most cities implement a **city-manager**/**council-manager** form of government where the people elect a city council and a mayor. The mayor often is just a symbolic figure and has very little real power. Collectively the mayor and the council hire a **city manager** to oversee the function of the city, while the mayor and council oversee long-term goals. While having an unelected official allows that position to have less incentive in just pleasing the voters, it has possibility for corruption similar to the **machine politics** of the 19th century

Cities can also implement a **council-mayor** system where the mayor has more executive abilities, varying from **weak-mayor systems** to **strong-mayor systems**

Due to urban centers often crossing county barriers, a large amount of local government is spend with these overlapping jurisdictions

**Roll-off** is the term for when voters may vote for larger positions and not vote for smaller more local positions

**Zoning** laws regulate what types of buildings can be built on what property, and are a key factor in the ever growing expense of housing
