It seems like naturalism struggles to explain how things came to be ([[The Cosmological Argument]], [[Fine-Tuning of the Universe]]) and there's good historical evidence for Christianity ([[Miracles]], [[The Resurrection]]). However at the same time, perhaps we shouldn't expect to be able to know how things came to be without going outside the system (which we can't really), and there seems to be some problems on Christianity as well ([[Divine Hiddenness]], [[Sovereignty vs Free Will]])

Assuming there is an infinite future (at least in Heaven), if God exists and all of time is equally present to him (else his understanding changes with time), then God has created an actual infinite. Because of that, is a past actual infinite really impossible?

The Catholic Church affirms that God's existence "can be known with certainty from the created world by the natural light of human reason" - what about someone who hasn't heard the Gospel and only knows enough about physics to hypothesize that naturalism could be a fine hypothesis

The evolutionary argument works so well because theists can say that we can truly trust our reasoning - but can we? What about Descartes?

At the same time everyone feels a so called "yearning" for God, everyone also experiences doubt - is that expected as well under Theism?

Do the number of [[UFO Sightings]] diminish evidence of eye-witness apparitions?

Do conjoined twins pose any problems for Catholic teachings?

[[Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem]] along with self-reference seem to be powerful ideas
