## War and Human Nature

Some interesting questions arise if you think about whether you really want peace in the world, or to have no regrets. Since if there are no wars, then what is enforcing that? It might be worse than what it is like with wars. And if you don't experience the lows, how can you experience the highs?

Countries are an interesting subject. China is becoming a new world superpower, with the potential to outclass the US, which has been the biggest kid on the block for longer than anyone alive on earth. That's crazy to think about. I believe Elon said since sometime in the 1890s or so. It can't last forever right? The Mongols are gone, the British are vastly diminished, perhaps the US is next. But both the US and China are unique in the sense that they focus on themselves a lot. After WWII, the US could have taken over the world, at the very least any country they wanted. They had more manpower, more nukes, more industry than anyone else. Yet they didn't and they helped rebuild Germany, rebuild Italy, and rebuild Japan.

It's a good thing that China is focused on itself, well and Taiwan, but they aren't really focused on taking over the world. At least to our knowledge. But they do have a lot of smart people doing amazing things, as evident in their cities and railway systems.
## Aliens and God

The classic Fermi paradox came up: where are the aliens? Perhaps every other species was single planetary and was wiped out. Elon argues that since we have the chance to expand, we should. This opportunity that we have won't be around forever. At the very least the sun will expand in half a billion years or so and make Earth like Venus. So we should work hard to expand while we can.

It's the age old question of how the universe works. Perhaps God is real and religion is right. Or perhaps we're in some sort of simulation where aliens are trying to figure out what happens when life develops independently. He talked about how SpaceX runs simulations all the time, since when you launch a rocket it's expensive, you don't get that many chances so you want it to go as well as it can. But if God isn't the answer, or at least not in the way that we think, we are likely missing something big. We understand a lot about physics to do things relevant to us on Earth, but we don't understand everything. There's still some missing pieces to the puzzle that we haven't solved. And this is where AI can come in, since if we humans can advance physics and discover new things, just think about what AI can do.
## AI and X

With any technology, you first try to get it to work and then try to make it efficient. Reminds me of nuclear fusion. This makes sense for Tesla autopilot that only has around 100 watts on Tesla Cars. It has to be efficient, as they want it to be compatible with the hardware that they have sold to their customers, even though they are updating it regularly.

xAI released their new model Grok which is aimed to be a truth seeking LLM that is able to answer "spicier" questions and isn't afraid to make some jokes. It has constant access to X making it the only LLM at the moment besides perhaps ChatGPT search with Bing that can provide real time information. He talked about the X algorithm as well, and how its important to keep working on it. Ideally, for recommended content, everything should be vectorized and compared with a user's vector, or one of a user's vectors to see if it's compatible. This is not at all a new idea in the field of AI, but it would be cool and useful to see it implemented on X. And hopefully other social media or news sites do that as well.
