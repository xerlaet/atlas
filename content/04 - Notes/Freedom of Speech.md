## Key Terms
- **First Amendment:** A fundamental right in the U.S. Constitution that protects freedom of speech and the press.
- **Communications Paradigms:** Different models or frameworks for communication, such as print, broadcast, and the Internet, which have historically been subject to varying degrees of regulation.
- **Chilling Effect:** When a government action or law causes people to avoid engaging in legal speech and publication out of fear of prosecution, thereby inhibiting freedom of expression.
- **Obscenity:** A legal category of material that is not protected by the First Amendment, determined by a three-part guideline established in _Miller v. California_.
- **Community Standards:** A criterion used in determining whether material is obscene, based on the prevailing standards of the local community. This concept became complex with the advent of the Internet's lack of physical location.
- **Communications Decency Act (CDA) of 1996:** An early U.S. federal law aimed at censoring indecent material on the Internet, much of which was ruled unconstitutional.
- **Least Restrictive Means:** A legal principle stating that when the government pursues a legitimate goal that might infringe on free speech, it must use the least restrictive method to accomplish that goal.
- **Children’s Internet Protection Act (CIPA):** A U.S. federal law requiring libraries and schools that receive certain federal funds to install filter software on Internet terminals.
- **Child Pornography:** Images or videos depicting actual minors engaged in sexually explicit conduct. Laws against child pornography focus on the abuse and exploitation of children in its production.
- **Sexting:** Sending sexually suggestive or explicit text messages or photos, usually by cellphone or social media, often involving minors.
- **Spam:** Mass, unsolicited email, which can include commercial advertising, political messages, scams, and other content.
- **Anonymous Remailer Services:** Online services that allow users to send email without revealing their true identity by stripping off return addresses and potentially routing messages through multiple points.
- **Net Neutrality:** Proposals for regulations concerning how telephone and cable companies that provide Internet access interact with their broadband customers and set charges for services, particularly regarding content discrimination and tiered service.
- **Tiered Service:** Offering different levels of speed and priority for Internet traffic at different price points.
- **Prior Restraint:** Government action that prohibits speech or expression before it takes place, generally considered a severe violation of the First Amendment.
