# Argument from Desire
---

1. All natural desires have a natural object (e.g. hunger to food)
2. Humans have a desire for perfect joy, meaning, and beauty which cannot be fully satisfied by the material world
3. Therefore there must be something beyond the natural world to satisfy this desire

## Analysis
Humans desire imaginary things all the time. Someone might want to fly like Superman, travel through time, or own a billion dollars. That doesn't mean Superman is real.

Perhaps there should be a distinction between artificial/learned desires (wanting to fly, owning a Ferrari) and natural/innate desires (hunger, companionship). The artificial ones don't have to correspond with reality, but the natural ones do.

Evolution would likely select for organisms that are never fully satisfied in order to keep competing. Critics of this say that this feeling hits hardest when someone is insanely rich and has achieved all earthly goals.

Even if we do have natural desires, it doesn't mean they get fulfilled. A man in the desert might starve to death, similarly a human may never get that perfect fulfillment they are looking for.

## Stance
I think that some humans can be very satisfied with strong friendships, a meaningful job/hobbies, and enough wealth to not have to worry about their basic needs for themselves or the people around them.

Is there an even deeper desire though? There might very well be, but I don't really think it's a great argument to say oh well now God has to exist to fulfill that. I can see it being likely that this deep desire is part of the artificial category, or just often goes unfulfilled.
