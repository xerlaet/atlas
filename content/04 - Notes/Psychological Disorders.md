## Definition and Diagnosis

A **psychological disorder** is a syndrome characterized by clinically significant disturbance in an individual's cognition, emotion regulation, or behavior that reflects a dysfunction in the psychological, biological, or developmental processes underlying mental functioning

In other words, there has to be *significant disturbances* in thoughts, feelings, and behaviors that reflect some kind of biological, psychological, or developmental *dysfunction*, leading to *significant distress or disability* in one's life, and *do not reflect expected or culturally approved responses* to certain events

**Psychopathology** is the study of psychological disorders, including their **etiology** (causes)

Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) <- now on the 5th version

The **diathesis-stress model** views a mental disorder happening as a combination of biological reasons along with stress

often comorbid with other disorders

### Social Anxiety disorder
extreme and persistent fear or anxiety of social situations that could be judged negatively by others
### Panic disorder
recurrent and unexpected panic attacks, and constant concern over more panic attacks

the thought of having more panic attacks can lead to a panic attack in and of itself

a region of the brain called the *locus coeruleus* is thought be involved in it

### Generalized anxiety disorder
a continuous state of excessive, uncontrollable, and pointless worry and apprehension

about 5.7% of the US population will develop symptoms at some point during their lifetime
## Obsessive-Compulsive and related disorders
generally involves intrusive, unpleasant thoughts and repetitive behaviors to the point where they disrupt daily life

studies show that there is a moderate genetic component, believed to be a part of the *orbitofrontal cortex* 
### Obsessive compulsive disorder (OCD)
thoughts and urges that are unwanted and make the person engage in repetitive behaviors or mental acts, e.g. spend hours washing hands or constantly rechecking if a light was turned off

common obsessions include concerns about germs and contamination, order and symmetry, and aggressive or lustful urges
### Body dysmorphic disorder
a perceived flaw in physical appearance that is either nonexistent or barely noticeable to other people, making the person think they are unattractive, ugly, hideous, or deformed

this can make a person constantly check the mirror or want to undergo surgery
### Hoarding disorder
someone who cannot bear to part with personal possessions, regardless of how valueless or useless these possessions are

usually this results in an accumulation of useless things
## Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
caused by extremely stressful or traumatic events, such as combat, natural disasters, and crimes, and is the only disorder in where a cause is explicitly defined

symptoms include *flashbacks* where the person behaves as if they are still at that moment, avoidance of related stimuli, negative emotional states, feelings of detachment, irritability

about 7% of adults in the US will experience PTSD in their lifetime, and factors such as the severity of the trauma, life stress, and immediate social support can influence the likelihood of developing it
## Mood disorders
mood is like winter, emotion is like a cold day

**Major depressive disorder**: symptoms for most days for at least 2 weeks
**Chronic depressive disorder**: chronic, low grade for 2 years or more
**Bipolar disorder**: depressive episodes paired with mania, perhaps *flight of ideas*
**Cyclothymic disorder**: chronic, low grade and quicker mood changes

### Suicide
when extreme emotion pain becomes unbearable
90% of those who commit suicide have at least one mental disorder, mood disorders being the most frequent
they tend to have a contagious effect, hearing about someone else's suicide increases the risk of another's suicide
## Schizophrenia
it is one disorder, but there are many symptoms/possibilities for it
characterized by major disturbances in thought, perception, emotion, and behavior, essentially disconnected from the world
about 1% of the population experiences it once during their lifetime

Positive symptoms
- delusions (could be paranoid, grandiose, or somatic)
- hallucinations
- disorganized thoughts and speech
- disorganized or catatonic behavior
- inappropriate affect

Negative symptoms
- flat affect
- alogia: reduced speech output
- avolition: lack of motivation
- anhedonia: inability to experience pleasure
- associality: social withdrawal

Cognitive symptoms
- poor executive function
- inability to sustain attention
- difficulty retaining and using information

In the past schizophrenia had different types that could be diagnosed, right now it is just considered one diagnosis even though there are many different symptoms and possibilities

Phases: prodrome, acute, and recovery
## Dissociative disorders
not remembering
extreme and frequent disruptions of awareness, memory, and personal identity

dissociative amnesia compared to retrograde amnesia, anterograde amnesia, infant amnesia

**Dissociative amnesia**: temporary loss of memory triggered by stress or trauma
**Dissociative amnesia with dissociative fugue**: travel/wandering and you forget who you are
**Depersonalization/derealization disorder**: depersonalization, detachment from oneself or surroundings
**Dissociative identity disorder (DID)**: gaps in memory, presence of alters, controversial because of the sudden appearance of the disorder commonly

## Childhood disorders
### Attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)
someone has trouble concentrating, are fidgety, restless, socially disruptive, and have trouble with impulse control

occurs in about 8% of children

if ADHD persists, then it can lead to long-term worse outcomes such as worse education, lower socioeconomic status, more likely unemployed, make less money, more likely to be divorced, etc.
### Autism Spectrum disorder
often misunderstood, it is characterized by deficits in social interaction, deficits in communication, and repetitive patterns of behavior or interests
1 in 59 children have it

## Personality disorders
personality disorders are where a person exhibits a personality style different significantly from expectations of their culture, and it is pervasive and inflexible

10 different disorders are recognized, organized into three clusters, cluster A being personality that is odd or eccentric, cluster B being impulsive, over dramatic and emotional, and cluster C being nervous and fearful

| Paranoid             | mistrust of others, takes offense easily                           | A       |
| Schizoid             | lacks interest with others, aloof and indifferent                  | A       |
| Schizotypal          | eccentricities in thought, unusual experiences                     | A       |
| Antisocial           | often lies and fights, irresponsible and lacks feelings for others | B       |
| Histrionic           | overly emotional and theatrical, emotions often shifting           | B       |
| Narcissistic         | overinflated sense of self-importance, impulsive and unpredictable | B       |
| Borderline           | unstable in self-image and mood, can't tolerate being alone        | B       |
| Avoidant             | socially inhibited and oversensitive to negative evaluation        | C       |
| Dependent            | submissive, clingy, and fears separation, helpless when alone      | C       |
| Obsessive-Compulsive | pervasive need for perfectionism, rigid, inflexible, and stubborn  | C       |
