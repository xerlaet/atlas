## Key Terms
- **Digital Divide:** The disparity in access to and regular use of modern information technology between different groups of people.
- **Neo-Luddites:** Individuals who reject or are highly critical of modern technology, often seeing it as harmful to quality of life, society, and the environment. Named after the original Luddites who protested against textile machinery in the early 19th century.
- **Computer Model:** A collection of data and equations used to describe or simulate the characteristics and behavior of a physical or intangible system.
- **Life Cycle Analysis:** A type of computer model that attempts to consider the resource use and environmental effects of all aspects of a product, including manufacture, use, and disposal.
- **Finite-Element Method:** A technique used in computer models, such as car crash analysis programs, that divides an object into a finite number of small pieces (elements) to calculate forces, stresses, and strains.
- **Technological Singularity:** The hypothetical future point at which artificial intelligence or combined human–machine intelligence surpasses human comprehension.
- **Greenhouse Effect:** The process by which gases in the earth's atmosphere trap heat, warming the planet. Water vapor and carbon dioxide are key greenhouse gases.
- **Wisdom of the Crowd:** The idea that the collective knowledge and opinions of a large group of diverse individuals can be more accurate or insightful than that of a single expert.
- **Content Creators:** Internet users who produce their own online material, such as blogs, videos, or reviews, as opposed to solely consuming content.
- **Have-Laters:** A term used to suggest that disparities in technology access are temporary, with technologies becoming cheaper and more widely available over time, rather than creating a permanent divide between "haves" and "have-nots."
