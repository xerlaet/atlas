# Ch 1 Main Principles

## Style
Professional and technical writing should be simple and easy to understand. Short concise sentences are easiest to digest, and flowery language should be avoided.

It should be objective and give a complete picture of options available to the reader and utilize credible sources.

Think of a spectrum where the right is subjective and the left is objective, presenting a technical solution to a client would be in the middle, however for other things it should be on the left.

### Voice
Active voice is better (subject is noted first then the action), however if the doer does not matter passive voice can be ok to break monotony

Also it can be used to tone down a message, e.g. "the parts were manufactured poorly" instead of "you manufactured the parts poorly"

### Useful Tips
Think about what is important to the reader not yourself

Since the goal of professional communication is the greatest possible understanding, visuals are very helpful

### Structure
Design for clear navigation with clear headers in an organized fashion. Key messages should be at the beginning and end of paragraphs and the document

Most documents include an introduction, a body, and a conclusion. The introduction should include the purpose and context for the reader and be a forecast of what is to follow. The body should address the main topics in detail with supporting facts etc. following any order set in the introduction. The conclusion should summarize and reiterate the key points.

### Editing and Grammar
Always leave time to edit a rough draft and don't always rely on grammar or spell checkers

The most common mistakes are grammatical errors. Common ones include subject verb agreement, articles (a, an, the), commas/semicolons/colons, run on sentences, gerunds, pronouns, sentence structure. Gerunds are nouns masquerading as verbs, such as "i like writing reports" where "writing" (commonly -ing words) are gerunds.

### Etiquette
Having proper etiquette is important in the workplace because bosses will expect it. It's a good default

## Ethics
Be ethical (look at the standards published by organizations). Also give proper attribution to sources that you use, and respect IP.

Respect noncompetition and nondisclosure agreements

Whistleblowers should be protected by law, but worst case (being fired hopefully) is better than continuing to work there

### IEEE Ethical Statement
Overall, software engineers should:
1. PUBLIC - Act with the best interest of the public (avoid dangers, improve quality of life)
2. CLIENT AND EMPLOYEER - Act with the best interest of the client and employer (confidentiality, follow IP law)
3. PRODUCT - Ensure their products meet the highest professional standards (be qualified, follow standards, documentation)
4. JUDGEMENT - Be independent in professional judgement (objective evaluations when asked for, disclose conflicts of interests)
5. MANAGEMENT - Promote ethical maintenance of software (give realistic estimates, ensure policies and procedures are known ahead of time)
6. PROFESSION - Advance the integrity and reputation of the profession (promote public knowledge, and that others follow the code)
7. COLLEAGUES - Be fair and supportive of colleagues (give credit, listen to colleagues)
8. SELF - Participate in lifelong learning (improve your own abilities and knowledge)

# Ch 6 Document Design
Readers typically read a new document in an "F" pattern, so it makes sense to put the most important information there

Margins are always 1" with an exception for Resumes

A heading standing alone without text is a "widow" and should be avoided using page breaks. Font size for print documents should typically be 10 to 12 points

Sans serif fonts don't have tails (arial, calibri) as opposed to serif fonts which do (times new roman, cambria). Generally sans serif fonts are more readable

All caps is generally used for warnings or other legally binding duties. Outside of this however all caps is generally taken as the writer shouting

The standard for line spacing is 1.0 with 0 pt. before and after

Pictures are worth a thousand words, but make sure to caption any illustration and explain its significance in the document text
