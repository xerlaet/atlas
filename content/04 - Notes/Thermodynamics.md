# Thermodynamics
---

**Thermodynamics** is the branch of physics that deals with heat, work, and temperature, and their relation to energy, radiation, and the physical properties of matter. It is governed by four fundamental laws that describe how energy is transferred and transformed in a physical system.

## The Laws of Thermodynamics
These laws define the core principles of energy conversion and thermal equilibrium.
- **Zeroth Law**: If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This law defines temperature.
- **First Law**: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or changed from one form to another. It is a statement of the conservation of energy.
- **Second Law**: The total entropy of an isolated system can only increase over time. It establishes the "arrow of time" and explains why heat naturally flows from hot to cold objects.
- **Third Law**: The entropy of a system approaches a constant minimum as the temperature approaches absolute zero.

## Key Equations
The First Law and the classical definition of entropy change are central to all thermodynamic calculations.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U &= Q - W && \text{(First Law)} \\
\Delta S &= \int \frac{dQ_{rev}}{T} && \text{(Entropy Change)}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here, $U$ is internal energy, $Q$ is heat added, $W$ is work done by the system, and $S$ is entropy.
