# Logic
---

**Logic** is the formal study of reasoning and inference, providing a mathematical framework for evaluating the structure of arguments. 

## Core Principles
- **Validity**: An argument where the conclusion logically follows from the premises.
- **Soundness**: An argument that is formally valid *and* has objectively true premises.
- [Modes of Reasoning](/notes/modes-of-reasoning) - Deduction, induction, and abduction

## Classical Logic
Focuses on binary truth values (strictly true or false) and absolute certainty.
- [Propositional Logic](/notes/propositional-logic) - Basic true/false statement connectives
- [Logical Equivalences](/notes/logical-equivalences) - Rules for statement transformation
- [Rules of Inference](/notes/rules-of-inference) - Templates for valid logical deductions
- [First-Order Logic](/notes/first-order-logic) - Objects, predicates, and relations
- [Quantifiers](/notes/quantifiers) - Universal and existential scope

## Non-Classical Logic
Extensions or deviations from classical logic to handle uncertainty, time, or modality.
- [Fuzzy Logic](/notes/fuzzy-logic) - Continuous truth degrees (0 to 1)
- [Modal Logic](/notes/modal-logic) - Necessity and possibility evaluation
- [Deontic Logic](/notes/deontic-logic) - Moral obligation and permission
