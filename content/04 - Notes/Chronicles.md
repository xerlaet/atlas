## 1-9: Genealogies
- Lists from Adam to the return from exile (1 Chr 1–9).  
- Establishes Israel’s history and tribal identity.  
- Emphasizes the lineage of David and the tribes of Israel.

## 10-29: Stories about David
**David as King**  
- David anointed, rules Judah then all Israel (1 Chr 11–12).  
- Conquers Jerusalem and makes it the capital (1 Chr 11).  

**Temple Preparations**  
- Plans for the temple, organizes priests and Levites (1 Chr 22–29).  
- David gathers materials and gives instructions to Solomon.  

**Military and Administration**  
- Registers troops, organizes governance (1 Chr 14–27).  
- David expands Israel’s power and secures borders.  

**David’s Legacy**  
- Prepares for temple construction and orderly worship.  
- Passes leadership to Solomon (1 Chr 29).  

## 1-36: Kings in Jerusalem
**Solomon’s Reign**  
- Builds the temple, reign marked by wealth and wisdom (2 Chr 1–9).  

**Divided Kingdom Focus on Judah**  
- Chronicles focuses on Judah, Davidic kings, and temple worship (2 Chr 10–36).  
- Faithful kings: Asa, Jehoshaphat, Hezekiah, Josiah.  
- Unfaithful kings: Rehoboam, Jehoram, Manasseh.  

**Prophets and Reforms**  
- Prophets call kings to covenant faithfulness.  
- Temple reforms and revival movements recorded.  

**Exile**  
- Judah eventually falls to Babylon (2 Chr 36).  
- Temple destroyed, people exiled; God’s promises preserved for the remnant.  

## Core Theological Takeaways
- God preserves the **Davidic line and temple worship**.  
- Faithfulness brings stability; idolatry leads to judgment.  
- Chronicles emphasizes **prayer, worship, and covenant obedience**.  
- God’s promises endure beyond exile → foreshadows restoration and Christ.
