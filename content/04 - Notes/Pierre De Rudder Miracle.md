# Pierre De Rudder Miracle
---

In 1867, a Belgian agricultural worker named Pierre De Rudder had his left leg crushed by a falling tree, resulting in a severe compound fracture.

For eight years, the bone completely refused to heal, ravaged by gangrene and necrosis, and the bone ends were visible. Multiple doctors examined him repeatedly, concluding it was incurable and strongly advised amputation to which Dr Rudder refused.

In 1875, he traveled to the Shrine of Our Lady of Lourdes in Oostakker, Belgium. While sitting on a bench praying, he reported feeling a sudden, intense sensation, and stood up and walked around. The open wound had completely closed leaving only scarred skin and the bones were rigidly fused.

When he died 23 years layer, a team of doctors examined his leg and found a massive, highly unusual bone callus perfectly bridging the gap where the bone had been shattered, firmly fusing the tibia and fibula together.

## Skeptical Response
There might be a timeline gap. Perhaps the bone was in a state of delayed union where the body was slowly forming a cartilage callus around the break. Eventually, the cartilage began to ossify (turn to hard bone), and his trip to the shrine coincided with the final stages where his bone could bear his weight again. Also if it was healed supernaturally, why in that strange way where there was a bone callus bridging the gap?

Dr. Affenaer, the physician who supposedly verified the leg was broken and rotting, had not examined De Rudder for several months prior to his trip, so it could have healed substantially over thos unobserved months.

The counter is that apparently there is testimony of people on the train with him who testified that his leg was still heavily bandaged, bleeding, and dangling just hours before the healing.

The best natural explanation seems to be a prolonged healing, however to claim that you have to discredit the witnesses from the train and at the shrine the day of.
