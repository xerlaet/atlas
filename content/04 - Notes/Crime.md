# Crime
---

## Key Terms
- **Hacking:** Intentional, unauthorized access to computer systems, ranging from minor pranks to large-scale theft and disruption.
- **Phone phreaking:** Manipulating the telephone system, a practice associated with hacking in Phase 2.
- **Social engineering:** Fooling people into disclosing sensitive information, such as passwords, a technique used by hackers.
- **Internet Worm:** A program released in 1988 that spread quickly on the Internet, jamming computers and disrupting processing.
- **Virus:** A type of malicious program that can spread, destroy files, steal passwords, and disrupt computer systems.
- **Denial-of-service attacks:** Overloading a target website with numerous requests, causing it to slow down or shut down.
- **Zombies:** Infected computers remotely controlled by a person who distributed a virus, often used to send spam or participate in other malicious activities.
- **Hacktivism:** The use of hacking to promote a political cause.
- **White hat hacker:** A hacker who uses their skills to demonstrate system vulnerabilities and improve security.
- **Black hat hacker:** A hacker who uses their skills for malicious or criminal purposes.
- **Gray hat:** A hacker who uses methods of questionable legality or publicizes vulnerabilities before informing system owners.
- **Computer forensics (Digital forensics):** The field of collecting evidence from computer files and disks for legal purposes.
- **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA):** The main federal law in the United States addressing computer crimes, making unauthorized access illegal.
- **Identity theft:** Crimes where a criminal uses the identity of an unknowing innocent person for financial gain.
- **Phishing:** Fraudulent spam (usually email) sent to millions of people, attempting to trick them into revealing personal information.
- **Smishing:** Phishing scams conducted through text messaging (SMS).
- **Vishing:** Voice phishing, where criminals provide a phone number for victims to call and reveal personal information.
- **Pharming:** A technique to lure people to fake websites by planting false Internet addresses in Domain Name Servers (DNS).
- **Skimmers:** Recording devices surreptitiously installed in card readers to steal credit or debit card numbers and PINs.
- **Biometrics:** Biological characteristics unique to an individual (e.g., fingerprints, eye patterns) used for identification.
- **Geolocation software:** Software used to determine the physical location of website visitors.
- **Libel:** Written defamation, saying something false and damaging to someone's reputation in print or other media.
- **Slander:** Verbal defamation.
- **Libel tourism:** The phenomenon of bringing libel lawsuits in jurisdictions with weaker protections for freedom of speech, even if the parties and content are primarily located elsewhere.
- **Authority-to-prevent-entry principle:** An alternative principle for governing online activities across borders, suggesting a government can block illegal material within its borders but not apply its laws to activities legal in another country.
