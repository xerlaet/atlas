# Tixtla Eucharistic Miracle
---
![Pasted image 20260927202909.png](/attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260927202909.png)
On October 21, 2006, at St. Martin of Tours in Tixtla, Mexico, a religious sister noticed that one of the consecrated hosts in her ciborium had begun to secrete a reddish substance.

After three years of keeping the host secure, Bishop Zavala Castro authorized a scientific investigation from 2009 to 2012 led by Dr. Ricardo Castañón Gómez. According to his team, the following was found:
1. The reddish substance was human blood containing hemoglobin and human DNA, and the tissue was identified as myocardium
2. Microscopic analysis purportedly proved that the blood originated from the inside out. While the outer layer had coagulated, internal layers remained fresh
3. The internal layers contained intact white blood cells, red blood cells, and active macrophages
4. The blood type was identified as AB

The findings were released at a Catholic symposium and in books by Dr. Castañón. This includes highly magnified photographs claimed to be human cells, alongside a summary of laboratory test results. However, the reports omitted standard experimental controls and exact methodologies.

## Analysis
**Lack of DNA Concentration**: Only a trace amount of human DNA was recovered, which critics argue points directly to "touch DNA" left behind by human handling over the years.

**Missing Muscle Markers**: Independent reviewers (like Dr. Kelly Kearse) noted that according to the data, the sample tested negative for some chemical markers unique to human muscle tissue.

**Plant Cells**: The data also acknowledges the presence of plant cells in the sample, which aligns with a degraded wheat wafer rather than a pure heart.

## Skeptical Theory
Micro-condensation or a localized drop of moisture accumulated on a host several days prior. The supposed bleeding was actually *Serratia marcescens* (a bacteria known to produce a vivid, blood-red pigment).

Degrading bread gluten will form stringy fibers that can easily be mistaken for muscle under a microscope. Furthermore, Environmental yeast and fungal spores (which are round, cellular structures) were likely misidentified as red and white blood cells. Finally, tests for hemoglobin and antibodies triggered false positives by reacting to similar proteins produced by the bacteria, unchecked by proper negative controls.
