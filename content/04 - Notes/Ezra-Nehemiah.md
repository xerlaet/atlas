# Ezra-Nehemiah
---

## 1-6: Zerubbabel
**Return from Exile**  
- Cyrus allows Jews to return to Jerusalem (Ezra 1).  
- Zerubbabel leads first group, rebuilds the temple foundation (Ezra 2–3).  

**Temple Construction**  
- Work resumes despite opposition (Ezra 4–5).  
- Temple completed and dedicated (Ezra 6).  

## 7-10: Ezra
**Ezra’s Mission**  
- Ezra leads a second wave, focusing on the law (Ezra 7).  
- Teaches the Torah and restores covenant faithfulness (Ezra 8–10).  

**Reforms**  
- Corrects intermarriage and covenant violations.  
- Emphasizes obedience to God’s law.  

## 1-7: Nehemiah
**Rebuilding the Walls**  
- Nehemiah obtains permission from Persian king (Neh 1–2).  
- Leads reconstruction of Jerusalem’s walls amid opposition (Neh 3–6).  

**Governance and Renewal**  
- Reestablishes community order and justice.  
- Organizes families and temple workers (Neh 7).  

## 8-12: Spiritual Renewal
**Public Reading of the Law**  
- Ezra reads the law to the people (Neh 8).  
- People repent, celebrate Feast of Booths, renew covenant.  

**Restoration of Worship**  
- Reforms in leadership, worship, and tithing (Neh 9–10).  
- Dedication of walls and renewed covenant community (Neh 12).  

## 13: Nope
- Chronicles further reforms and vigilance against intermarriage.  
- Shows ongoing struggle to maintain covenant faithfulness.  

## Core Theological Takeaways
- God **restores His people** even after exile.  
- Covenant obedience and worship are central to community life.  
- Leadership matters: faithful leaders guide both spiritual and physical restoration.  
- Renewal requires repentance, teaching, and committed action.
