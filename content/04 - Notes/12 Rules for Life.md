Animals are ruthless and fight for territory, especially lobsters. Their brains are rewired when they lose a fight making them more likely to lose again. Serotonin (Prozac) would alleviate that.

The principle of unequal distribution in wealth, scientific papers, musicians, etc. Four classical composers wrote almost all music performed today - prices law/pareto distribution

Waking up consistently and eating a healthy breakfast with fat and protein is often enough to reduce extreme stress in a person

Alcohol is an example of a positive feedback loop, as the medicine causes the disease

## 2. Treat yourself like someone you are responsible for helping
People often struggle taking prescribed medication to its completion, the funny thing is that if a person is tasked with giving medication for someone else even their pet their chances of following through with every dose increases as opposed to doing it for themselves only.

Many of the world's traditions regard the suffering attendant upon existence as the irreducible truth of Being

A possible way of reducing experience can be to order, chaos, and their interaction.

Chaos is ignorance, unexplored territory, the foreigner, the monster under the bed, despair and horror, where you end up when your dream dies and your career collapses, in short all situations we neither know nor understand.
Order is explored territory, the structure of society, the warm secure living room, the value of currency, the public facade we're called upon to wear, when things turn out the way we want them to.

Order is typically understood as masculine and the hierarchical structure of society, whereas chaos is typically understood as feminine and the birthplace of new ideas and creation. This juxtaposition of order and chaos has been ingrained in many societies, with the yin and yang, Osiris and Isis, Fuxi and Nuwa.

> An idea is more credible when it emerges as a consequence of investigations in different realms

> Order is not enough. You can't just be stable, and secure, and unchanging, because there are still vital and important new things to be learned. Nonetheless, chaos can be too much. You can't long tolerate being swamped and overwhelmed beyond your capacity to cope while you are learning what you still need to know. Thus, you need to place one foot in what you have mastered and understood and the other in what you are currently exploring and mastering. Then you have positioned yourself where the terror of existence is under control and you are secure, but where you are also alert and engaged. That is where there is something new to master and some way that you can be improved. That is where meaning is to be found.

Connection: straddling the edge, [[Test yourself at your Limit]] to grow (muscles, remembering)

The story of the Garden of Eden is about as old as stories go. A man is spurned by a woman, grows resentful and blames her, they are kicked out and have to mature like a child who grows up.

Peterson says the best definition of evil he can formulate is inflicting suffering for the sake of suffering, and that animals can't manage that. Except for the fact that they most definitely can: cats, dolphins, chimpanzees, probably many others. Nature without humans is still brutal and savage.

> Some people degenerate into the hell of resentment and the hatred of Being, but most refuse to do so, despite their suffering and disappointments and losses and inadequacies and ugliness, and again that is a miracle for those with the eyes to see it.

You need to determine where you are going, keep promises to and reward yourself, work work to bring about Heaven.

> Take care of yourself. Define who you are. Refine your personality. Choose your destination and articulate your Being. As the great nineteenth-century German philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche so brilliantly noted, "He whose life has a why can bear almost any how."

And whatever you choose, devote your life to it. That can give you Meaning? I've always thought of objective meaning having to come from a necessary being assigning a purpose, although perhaps that purpose is to find our own purpose?

## 3. Make friends with people who want the best for you
The easiest choice at any moment is to go downward in every sense of the word, yet in the long run it is nothing but hell

> But a villain who despairs of his villainy has not become a hero. A hero is something positive, not just the absence of evil.

If an unmotivated slacker is placed in the middle of a group of hard working intellectuals, the intellectuals will become more like the slacker, not the other way around.

> I am not saying that there is no hope of redemption. But it is much harder to extract someone from a chasm than to lift him from a ditch. And some chasms are very deep. And there's not much left of the body at the bottom.

Friendship is a reciprocal arrangement. If you have a friend whose friendship you wouldn't recommend to your sister, father, or son, why have such a friend for yourself? You are not morally obligated to support someone who is making the world a worse place.

## 4. Compare yourself to who you were yesterday, not to who someone else is today

> Failure is the price we pay for standards and, because mediocrity has consequences both real and harsh, standards are necessary

There will always be people better than you (in at least almost everything in the rare case you are a world expert in something that you have dedicated your life towards)

> As we mature we become, by contrast, increasingly individual and unique. The conditions of our lives become more and more personal and less and less comparable with those of others. Symbolically speaking, this means we must leave the house ruled by our father, and confront the chaos of our individual Being. We must take note of our disarray, without completely abandoning that father in the process. We must then rediscover the values of our culture—veiled from us by our ignorance, hidden in the dusty treasure-trove of the past—rescue them, and integrate them into our own lives. This is what gives existence its full and necessary meaning.

We are always moving from point a to point b, trying to make the world a better place
