# Shroud of Turin
---
![Pasted image 20260927214214.png](/attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260927214214.png)
The **Shroud of Turin** is arguably the most studied artifact in human history. It is a 14-foot linen cloth bearing the faint, negative image of a crucified man. For centuries it was venerated as the burial cloth of Jesus, but exploded into the scientific consciousness in 1898 when the photographer Secondo Pia took the first picture of it.

Here are some of the commonly cited properties:
1. The image on the cloth is a photographic negative. A 14th century medieval artist would not have conceived of, let alone painted, a perfect photographic negative 500 years before photography was invented
2. The shroud encodes 3D spatial depth through the light and dark values
3. The image is not made of paint, dye, or pigment. The discoloration is caused presumably by rapid, premature aging of the cellulose only .2 micrometers deep (top 2-4 fibers)

## Analysis
**Bishop's Memo**: A 1389 memorandum written by Bishop Pierre d'Arcis stated that he tracked down the artist who confessed to manufacturing the Shroud to draw pilgrims and money to the local church. Although he never claimed to have met the artist himself, and calling competing relics a "fake" was standard politics.

**Carbon-14 Dating**: A carbon-dating measurement by three labs was done in 1988 dating the shroud between 1260 and 1390, which matches the first time it appears in the historical record (1354 in Lirey, France). Believers have said that the samples could have been medieval patchwork, or heavily contaminated later on

**Anatomical Proportions**: Skeptics argue that the anatomical proportions prove it is a piece of art, being roughly eight heads tall with elongated forearms and fingers which matches Gothic art conventions of 14th century Europe. Although after crucifixion, the muscles might be locked resulting in the tenting affect allowing the hands to rest lower. The elongated fingers could be an illusion from the bones showing through the skin under intense radiation.

**X-ray Analysis**: There was an X-ray analysis done dating it to the 1st century. Although this is a novel method performed by a pro-Shroud advocate, and assumptions about temperature and humidity were made which might not be accurate.

**Failed Reproduction**: Luigi Garlaschelli tried to reproduce the shroud by placing a linen cloth over a volunteer, rubbing it with acidic pigment, aftificially aging it in an oven, and washing the pigment off. This resulting in the same discoloration of the fibers with a 3D negative image. Theists will point out though that the image was 40 fibers deep, not 4. Also, there was still wraparound distortion of the face. So to this day, no one has made a convincing copycat.

**DNA Contamination**: Modern genomic sequencing revealed a massive, complex archive of centuries of global handling. DNA matched lineages from Western Eurasia, the Middle East, and modern scientists.

**Mandylion Connection**: According to ancient Christian tradition, King Abgar V of Edessa was terminally ill and wrote to Jesus asking Him to come heal him. One way or another Jesus pressed his face to the cloth leaving an imprint of His features. The cloth was hidden for centuries to be protected from pagan persecutions, was rediscovered during a Persian siege in 544 and transferred to Constantinople in 944. It remained there until it vanished during the sacking of Constantinople in 1204. Some theists posit that the Mandylion of Edessa is actually the Shroud of Turin folded up to where only the face is visible. If so, it would push the historical record back to the time of Jesus, however it wouldn't be a burial cloth then.
