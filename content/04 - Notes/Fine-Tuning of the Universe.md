# Fine-Tuning of the Universe
---

There are 31 fundamental dimensionless physical constants that are not derived from any underlying mathematical theory, they are simply brute facts. If you change them ever so slightly, you usually get a dead universe:
- The cosmological constant which is tuned to 1 part in $10^{120}$
- If the strong nuclear force is 2% stronger, all hydrogen fuses into helium during the big bang. If it is 5% weaker, the universe contains nothing but hydrogen.
- The low entropy of the universe is tuned to 1 part in $10^{10^{123}}$

Possible explanations:
1. Chance
2. Physical necessity
3. Designer

A designer is the most likely explanation.

## Analysis
**Inverse Gambler's Fallacy**: If you walk into a casino and see someone roll 50 double-sixes in a row, you don't assume "oh there must be billions of other people rolling dice in the casino for this one guy to get so lucky". You assume the dice are loaded. Chance seems like a terrible answer to this.

A better version of the chance answer is to apply the [Anthropic Principle](/notes/anthropic-principle) under a [Multiverse](/notes/multiverse-theories) view. However multiverses have a couple problems of their own.

With physical necessity, perhaps there is an underlying law of physics that explains the constants. This requires either a lot of faith or a lot of skepticism in our current understanding of physics.

One final answer is to say that there simply is no fine tuning. No-one has actually simulated all of the constants changing at once (because it's really hard lol), but maybe that would reveal that there is no fine tuning. Although critics will say that we should limit the focus to the epistemically illuminated region that we can observe, and based on that fine tuning definitely is real.

Something else to consider is different ways that God could have created life. God could have paired each of our souls with a single electron if He wanted to. We need to do some philosophy and thinking into what God would want to do based on what we know about Him. [Skeptical Theism](/notes/skeptical-theism) actually says we don't know what God would do, so we can't say that the universe is fine tuned for life even though under naturalism it seems so unlikely.

## Stance

My best response would be to put faith into some sort of philosophical multiverse with an unknown explanation as to why we shouldn't believe we are Boltzmann brains and that we shouldn't believe the universe will devolve into complete chaos at the next moment. Or perhaps some sort of underlying physical law is more likely, we definitely are missing some things with dark matter and combining general relativity and quantum mechanics so perhaps there is something big we are missing. It's still a lot of speculation though.
