# Fine-Tuning of the Universe
---

There are 31 fundamental dimensionless physical constants that are not derived from any underlying mathematical theory, they are simply brute facts. If you change them ever so slightly, you usually get a dead universe:
- The cosmological constant which is tuned to 1 part in $10^{120}$
- If the strong nuclear force is 2% stronger, all hydrogen fuses into helium during the big bang. If it is 5% weaker, the universe contains nothing but hydrogen.
- The low entropy of the universe is tuned to 1 part in $10^{10^{123}}$

## Analysis
**Inverse Gambler's Fallacy**: If you walk into a casino and see someone roll 50 double-sixes in a row, you don't assume "oh there must be billions of other people rolling dice in the casino for this one guy to get so lucky". You assume the dice are loaded. This doesn't quite work if you apply the [Anthropic Principle](/notes/anthropic-principle) for a multiverse, but it is still a good consideration.

Is there a [Multiverse](/notes/multiverse-theories)?
Multiverse generating models often require fine tuning of their own so they just push the problem back a step. Here are some better formulations of a multiverse:
- everything possible exists
- every consistent mathematical structure describes a universe
- every logically consistent universe exists
- Steven Wolfram's [Theory of Everything](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2020/04/finally-we-may-have-a-path-to-the-fundamental-theory-of-physics-and-its-beautiful/)

In a multiverse, we should 1. expect to be Boltzmann brains which removes our ability to trust ourselves and 2. expect the universe to devolve into chaos at every moment which is just a crazy thing to have to believe

Additionally, while fine-tuning may be necessary for the life that we know of on our planet, perhaps other forms of life can exist in physical structures that we wouldn't think it could. At the very least, God would be able to make that work - to such a level that our conscious selves could be attached to singular electrons

Finally, future developments in science might change our understanding - perhaps a lot of constants are really just a few or have to be a certain way. If it turns out that there is more fine tuning then we think, the response doesn't change. If there is less, then the response gets stronger.

Perhaps limit the focus to the epistemically illuminated region that we can see since we haven't tried to simulate all of them varying yet
